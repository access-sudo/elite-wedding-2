A note from the atelier

We still plan weddings
with paper and a pencil.

Every celebration starts as a sheet of cartridge paper on a long table in Jaipur. We draw the light, the shade on the steps, where a grandmother will sit, and how a bride's veil will move when the gate opens. Only when the drawing feels right do we pick up a phone and start booking.

Nothing here is rendered by a machine. The sketches on this page are from our own books — annotated, erased, corrected in the margin, and eventually coloured in.

A pencil sketch of the day, drawn before anything is booked.

One atelier director from the first drawing to the last dance.

Twice-daily site walks in the final week, always on foot.

Meera Rathore

Founder & Atelier Director

Plate 01 — Baraat EntrancePencil & watercolour
01The Arrival

Baraat Entrance

Drums first. Then the light.

Every commission begins with the arrival. We draw the torana, set the dhol players two paces behind the groom, and place four hundred guests so that the first thing they see is a silhouette framed in gold leaf.

Torana GateDhol ProcessionTorch Line

the gate should feel like it was always there

Plate 02 — Haldi CourtyardPencil & watercolour
02The Blessing

Haldi Courtyard

Saffron light, brass, laughter.

Marigold canopies pull the afternoon into something golden. Brass urns are filled on site, low platforms are draped in raw silk, and the whole courtyard is pitched so the eldest aunt is never asked to stand.

Marigold CanopyBrass RitualShade Study

nine hundred kilos of marigold, refreshed at dawn

Plate 03 — Mehndi SalonPencil & watercolour
03The Lounge

Mehndi Salon

Where the evening slows down.

Floor seating, bolsters, and a henna medallion inlaid into the marble itself. Lanterns hang at eye level so the artists are lit like portraits, and a ghazal trio plays softer than the conversation.

Floor SeatingLive GhazalHenna Medallion

twelve artists, four hours, one unhurried flow

Plate 04 — Floral MandapPencil & watercolour
04The Vows

Floral Mandap

A canopy built around a flame.

Four ivory pillars, a dome of jasmine and rose, one fire at the centre. We draw the mandap around camera sightlines first, so the sacred flame never once crosses a mother's face during the vows.

Sacred FireJasmine DomeSightline Plan

twelve hundred rose stems per arch — twice a day

Plate 05 — Royal TablePencil & watercolour
05The Feast

Royal Table

Twelve courses, no waiting.

Long tables beneath hand-blown glass, a royal thali reimagined across seven counters. The brigade plates inside the hall so the last course arrives exactly as warm as the first — a promise we write into the plan.

Seven CountersLive PlatingHeritage Menu

sixty-two chefs, forty minute service window

Plate 06 — Entertainment StagePencil & watercolour
06The Night

Entertainment Stage

The courtyard becomes a club.

Tiered seating, an orchestra riser, and a lighting rig tuned to skin rather than screens. At midnight the drapery lifts and the courtyard turns into a dance floor that nobody wants to leave.

Live OrchestraLighting DesignAfter Party

sound balanced across fourteen hundred square metres
